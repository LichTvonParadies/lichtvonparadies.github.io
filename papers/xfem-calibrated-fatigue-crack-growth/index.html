Fatigue crack growth · 2026

A hybrid XFEM-calibrated analytical framework for efficient fatigue crack growth prediction

Sheng Zhang, Enhui Zhang, John Cookson, Pu Huang, Wenyi Yan
International Journal of Fatigue · Journal article

Publisher / DOI ↗Download citation

Abstract

Accurate fatigue crack growth (FCG) prediction in finite-thickness components requires representation of three-dimensional (3D) crack-front evolution and out-of-plane constraint effects that conventional two-dimensional solutions cannot capture. Crack propagation simulations using 3D extended finite element method (XFEM) can resolve these effects but remain computationally prohibitive for high-cycle fatigue applications. This paper proposes a hybrid XFEM-calibrated analytical framework for efficient FCG prediction under constant- and variable-amplitude cyclic loading. Stationary 3D XFEM analyses are performed at discrete crack configurations to determine the through-thickness stress intensity factor and the corresponding geometric factor β ( z ). Crack growth between calibration steps is then integrated analytically using established crack growth laws. A spatially varying constraint factor α ( z ) is introduced to account for the thickness-dependent variation in plastic zone size and to enable overload retardation modeling. The framework is experimentally validated using compact-tension specimens of Grade E cast steel and R400HT rail steel under stress ratios R = 0.1 – 0.5 and single-overload conditions (OLR = 1.5 – 2.0). Predicted crack growth lives agree with experiments within 7%, and the predicted crack-front evolution matches experimentally observed marker bands. Compared with full XFEM crack propagation, the proposed framework reduces computational time by more than an order of magnitude while retaining 3D crack-front representation. The proposed framework provides an efficient and physically consistent strategy for FCG prediction in configurations where closed-form solutions are unavailable or where load interaction effects are significant.

Research question

Full 3D XFEM crack propagation is computationally expensive, while conventional 2D analytical factors miss through-thickness crack-front and constraint effects.

Method

Periodically extract beta(z) from stationary 3D XFEM, integrate crack growth between calibrations, and introduce a spatial constraint factor alpha(z) for plastic-zone-based overload retardation models.

Contribution and comparison

Separates geometric calibration from cycle-by-cycle crack-growth integration while retaining a representation of 3D crack-front evolution and thickness-dependent constraint.

Scope and limitations

  • Validation is on CT specimens, not structural-scale components or all service spectra.
  • Mixed-mode loading and representative in-service spectrum loading are future extensions.
  • Crack-growth-law and retardation-model selection matters: NASGRO alone did not reproduce the reported single-overload retardation.
  • The 7% statement concerns total life, not every delay-cycle, crack-length or local crack-front error.
  • Classical analytical solutions remain very efficient for standard geometries under their valid assumptions.

Damage tolerance and structural integrity assessment; Efficient 3D fatigue crack growth analysis; Railway steel overload-retardation studies
