Fatigue crack growth · 2026

A unified framework for full-lifecycle fatigue crack evolution in hollow-section structures

Sheng Zhang, Enhui Zhang, John Cookson, Pu Huang, Wenyi Yan
Engineering Fracture Mechanics · Journal article

Publisher / DOI ↗Download citation

Abstract

Fatigue cracks in hollow-section structures undergo wall breakthrough and crack-front reconfiguration, yet these topology changes are often treated separately, limiting understanding of full-lifecycle crack evolution. This study develops a unified framework for fatigue crack growth (FCG) in hollow-section structures under realistic service loading. The framework enables continuous tracking of crack evolution across the three stages, with crack-topology transitions triggered automatically by geometry-based criteria. An XFEM-calibrated analytical method, combined with a global-local modelling strategy, is integrated to achieve computational efficiency by coupling periodic XFEM recalibration with analytical FCG integration. The results show strongly stage-dependent crack evolution. Stage I accounts for most of the total fatigue life. Stage II is brief but produces a pronounced amplification of the inner-surface crack-driving force because of constraint relaxation at wall breakthrough. Full-lifecycle simulations involving up to 124 million load cycles were completed within about four hours, demonstrating the computational efficiency of the framework. A key mechanistic finding is that the outer-surface crack half-length at wall breakthrough converges to a narrow range of 24–27 mm for the thickness studied, consistent with forensic observations. This convergence arises from Stage I crack-shape evolution toward an equilibrium aspect ratio and is governed primarily by geometric constraint rather than by the initial flaw morphology. These findings support a breakthrough-based fatigue assessment concept for the structure studied. While the quantitative values are case-specific, the proposed framework and the observed stage-wise behavior are applicable to analogous hollow-section structures.

Research overview

Fatigue cracks in a hollow wall do not remain in one geometric state. A surface crack can break through the wall, redistribute the crack-driving force and then evolve as a through-thickness crack. This paper connects those three stages in one framework, using geometry-based transitions, global-local finite element modelling and periodic XFEM calibration followed by analytical Paris-law integration under a service-load spectrum. In the studied rectangular railway drawbar, Stage I accounts for 82–96% of simulated total life across twelve cases. Simulations covering up to 124 million load cycles take about four hours on the reported setup. The predicted outer-surface crack half-length at breakthrough lies around 24–27 mm for the studied 18 mm wall and agrees with a forensic measurement. These findings support geometry-specific assessment; they do not establish a universal inspection interval or transferable breakthrough threshold.

研究介绍

空心壁面中的疲劳裂纹会经历表面裂纹扩展、壁厚突破后的重构以及贯穿裂纹扩展三个阶段，裂纹驱动力也随之重新分布。本研究利用几何判据触发阶段转换，结合整体—局部有限元建模、XFEM 周期标定和服务载荷谱下的 Paris 定律解析积分，将三阶段贯通起来。对于所研究的矩形空心铁路拉杆，12 个算例中第一阶段占模拟总寿命的 82%–96%；在报告的计算配置下，最多约 1.24 亿载荷循环的模拟耗时约四小时。对于该 18 mm 壁厚，预测的外表面突破裂纹半长约为 24–27 mm，与失效取样观测相符。这些结果用于支持特定几何条件下的评估，不构成通用检修间隔或可直接迁移的突破阈值。

Research question

Surface cracking, wall breakthrough and crack-front reconfiguration are often analysed separately, obscuring changes in crack-driving force and life partitioning across a hollow-section crack lifecycle.

Method

Use geometry-based transitions between surface-crack growth, post-breakthrough evolution and through-thickness propagation. A global-local model supplies stationary XFEM stress intensity factors at calibration states; analytical Paris-law integration advances growth between them under a measured service-load spectrum.

Contribution and comparison

Connects three crack topologies in one event-driven lifecycle description and examines life partitioning, crack-driving-force redistribution and breakthrough morphology for the studied railway drawbar. Forensic morphology agreement supports the breakthrough prediction; it does not independently validate every service-life prediction.

Scope and limitations

  • Quantitative validation is restricted to the flat wall of the studied rectangular hollow-section drawbar, its fixed 18 mm wall thickness, material and loading.
  • Paris-law integration omits crack closure and load-interaction effects, including overload-induced retardation.
  • Fixed XFEM calibration intervals may inadequately resolve rapid crack-driving-force changes near topology transitions; adaptive recalibration is future work.
  • Rounded-corner flaws and circular hollow sections require geometry-specific adaptation and validation.
  • The thickness-scaling relation is a testable hypothesis, not a validated transferable law. The reported maintenance threshold and inspection interval are case-specific.
  • Forensic comparison concerns breakthrough morphology; do not describe it as experimental validation of all predicted total lives.

Technical FAQ

What are the three crack-evolution stages?

Stage I is surface-crack growth; Stage II is post-breakthrough evolution with unequal inner- and outer-surface crack lengths; Stage III is subsequent through-thickness propagation after crack-front convergence. Geometry-based criteria trigger the transitions.

Source: Reviewed pre-proof PDF pp.4, 10–15; Sections 1–2.

How does the method reduce XFEM computation?

Stationary XFEM analyses recalibrate geometric factors at selected crack states. Analytical Paris-law integration advances growth between calibrations. Reported simulations span up to 124 million cycles in about four hours on the studied setup; this is not a hardware-independent speed guarantee.

Source: Reviewed pre-proof PDF pp.14–18, 27; Sections 2.5, 3.3.1 and 5.

Is the reported 24–27 mm value a crack length or a half-length?

It is the outer-surface crack half-length at wall breakthrough, for the studied 18 mm wall. It is not the total surface length, a crack depth, or a universal maintenance threshold.

Source: Reviewed pre-proof PDF pp.7, 21–22; Sections 2.1, 3.3.4 and 3.4.

Does less than 1.3% mean experimental prediction error?

No. It is the change in predicted life across the examined XFEM recalibration intervals of 0.8, 1, 1.5 and 2 mm. The forensic comparison supports breakthrough morphology; it does not validate every total-life prediction.

Source: Reviewed pre-proof PDF pp.18, 22; Sections 3.3.1 and 3.4.

Can the reported inspection interval be reused for another structure?

The reported 350,000 km interval is a case-specific interpretation for the studied drawbar, loading spectrum and material. Other thicknesses, corner flaws and circular hollow sections require geometry-specific adaptation and validation. The Paris-law model here omits load-interaction and crack-closure effects.

Source: Reviewed pre-proof PDF pp.26–28; Sections 4.3–5.

技术问答

三个裂纹演化阶段分别是什么？

第一阶段为表面裂纹扩展；第二阶段为壁厚突破后的重构，内外表面裂纹长度不相等；第三阶段为裂纹前缘趋于汇合后的贯穿扩展。阶段转换由几何判据触发。

Source: 已审阅预校样 PDF 第4、10–15页；第1–2节。

为什么可以减少 XFEM 计算量？

方法只在选定裂纹状态进行静态 XFEM 重标定，再在标定点之间进行 Paris 定律解析积分。报告配置下最多约1.24亿载荷循环耗时约四小时，不能据此保证所有硬件、几何和载荷的相同耗时。

Source: 已审阅预校样 PDF 第14–18、27页；第2、3.3.1、5节。

24–27 mm 是裂纹总长吗？

不是。这是所研究的18 mm壁厚下，壁厚突破时的外表面裂纹半长，不是总长、深度或通用检修阈值。

Source: 已审阅预校样 PDF 第7、21–22页；第2.1、3.3.4、3.4节。

小于1.3%是否代表实验预测误差？

不是。这是标定间隔取0.8、1、1.5、2 mm时预测寿命的数值敏感性。失效取样对比支持突破形貌，不能扩展为所有总寿命预测均通过实验验证。

Source: 已审阅预校样 PDF 第18、22页；第3.3.1、3.4节。

350,000 km检修间隔能直接用于其他拉杆吗？

不能直接迁移。该值针对研究中的几何、材料和载荷谱；其他壁厚、圆角缺陷和圆形空心截面需要专门适配与验证。本研究中的Paris模型未考虑载荷相互作用及裂纹闭合。

Source: 已审阅预校样 PDF 第26–28页；第4.3–5节。
