Fatigue crack growth · 2026

A unified framework for full-lifecycle fatigue crack evolution in hollow-section structures

Sheng Zhang, Enhui Zhang, John Cookson, Pu Huang, Wenyi Yan
Engineering Fracture Mechanics · Journal article

Publisher / DOI ↗Download citation

Abstract

Fatigue cracks in hollow-section structures undergo wall breakthrough and crack-front reconfiguration, yet these topology changes are often treated separately, limiting understanding of full-lifecycle crack evolution. This study develops a unified framework for fatigue crack growth (FCG) in hollow-section structures under realistic service loading. The framework enables continuous tracking of crack evolution across the three stages, with crack-topology transitions triggered automatically by geometry-based criteria. An XFEM-calibrated analytical method, combined with a global-local modelling strategy, is integrated to achieve computational efficiency by coupling periodic XFEM recalibration with analytical FCG integration. The results show strongly stage-dependent crack evolution. Stage I accounts for most of the total fatigue life. Stage II is brief but produces a pronounced amplification of the inner-surface crack-driving force because of constraint relaxation at wall breakthrough. Full-lifecycle simulations involving up to 124 million load cycles were completed within about four hours, demonstrating the computational efficiency of the framework. A key mechanistic finding is that the outer-surface crack half-length at wall breakthrough converges to a narrow range of 24–27 mm for the thickness studied, consistent with forensic observations. This convergence arises from Stage I crack-shape evolution toward an equilibrium aspect ratio and is governed primarily by geometric constraint rather than by the initial flaw morphology. These findings support a breakthrough-based fatigue assessment concept for the structure studied. While the quantitative values are case-specific, the proposed framework and the observed stage-wise behavior are applicable to analogous hollow-section structures.

Research question

Surface cracking, wall breakthrough and crack-front reconfiguration are often analysed separately, obscuring changes in crack-driving force and life partitioning across a hollow-section crack lifecycle.

Method

Use geometry-based transitions between surface-crack growth, post-breakthrough evolution and through-thickness propagation. A global-local model supplies stationary XFEM stress intensity factors at calibration states; analytical Paris-law integration advances growth between them under a measured service-load spectrum.

Contribution and comparison

Connects three crack topologies in one event-driven lifecycle description and examines life partitioning, crack-driving-force redistribution and breakthrough morphology for the studied railway drawbar. Forensic morphology agreement supports the breakthrough prediction; it does not independently validate every service-life prediction.

Scope and limitations

  • Quantitative validation is restricted to the flat wall of the studied rectangular hollow-section drawbar, its fixed 18 mm wall thickness, material and loading.
  • Paris-law integration omits crack closure and load-interaction effects, including overload-induced retardation.
  • Fixed XFEM calibration intervals may inadequately resolve rapid crack-driving-force changes near topology transitions; adaptive recalibration is future work.
  • Rounded-corner flaws and circular hollow sections require geometry-specific adaptation and validation.
  • The thickness-scaling relation is a testable hypothesis, not a validated transferable law. The reported maintenance threshold and inspection interval are case-specific.
  • Forensic comparison concerns breakthrough morphology; do not describe it as experimental validation of all predicted total lives.
